**Bear, round 1.** Two facts dominate the bear case and they are both in the fundamentals report. First, **TTM free cash flow has gone negative by approximately $24.7B** as capex runs at $50B for the year. Second, **total debt is approximately $124B**, with credit agencies on negative outlook, 5y CDS at highest since 2009, and a stated need to raise $45B to $50B in 2026. The bull thesis is "backlog converts cleanly to revenue and revenue converts cleanly to FCF". The bear thesis is "the bridge from RPO to FCF requires another 18 to 24 months of capex at this pace, during which time any disruption — credit market dislocation, GPU supply, customer concentration — directly threatens the equity at this multiple". The honest version of the bear case: this is not a story about Oracle's business being bad; it is a story about timing and balance sheet risk during the conversion period.

**Bull, round 2.** Acknowledged: the FCF conversion bridge is the real risk. Two countervailing data points. First, the company has disclosed that most of the recent RPO step up is on large AI contracts where the customer either prepays for GPU procurement or supplies the GPUs directly; this materially de risks the capex funding picture for those specific contracts. Second, the +84% OCI growth on a $4.9B quarterly base is itself evidence that conversion is already happening, not a future hope. The bear is right that the next 18 to 24 months are the capital intensive period; the bull says the data shows it is already working.

**Bear, round 2.** Acknowledged: the customer prepayment disclosure is the strongest single bull data point on safety, and OCI +84% is real. The counter is that customer prepayment dependence concentrates counterparty risk — if one or two of those large counterparties miss a payment milestone for any reason, the buildout pace and the equity multiple both compress. And the credit market is pricing this. CDS at 1.28% is not a trivial number for an investment grade name; the credit market is the most disciplined arbiter of balance sheet stress, and it is sending a clearer caution signal than the sell side. **Honest concession: if the next 4 quarters print conversion in line with backlog, the bear is wrong. The bear is not saying Oracle fails; the bear is saying the asymmetric risk at $176 with 4x leverage, negative FCF, and CDS at decade highs is to the downside on any execution wobble.**
